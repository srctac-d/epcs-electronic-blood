EPCS "Electronic Blood" System Architecture
1. Power & Thermal Management Dynamics

    Matching Charging Stacks to DC Power: Charging stacks are designed to directly match available direct current (DC) power sources without excessive conversion steps.

    Low-Voltage Cabinet-Level Stacks: Stacks operate at lower voltages directly matched to rack/cabinet power distribution requirements, reducing total internal heat generation.

    Transformer & RF Reduction: Eliminates reliance on heavy copper transformers inside individual cabinets, significantly dropping total facility heat output and lowering radio frequency (RF) interference.

    Flow-Driven Heat Dissipation: Electrolyte flow rates are calculated to exceed the thermal dissipation requirements of high-density server electronics, ensuring thermal headroom during peak loads.

2. Integrated Cooling Loops & Thermal Control

    Combined Heat Regulation: Uses passive storage tank walls combined with external/mounted radiators for primary heat dissipation.

    Secondary Return Line Refrigeration: Integrates a secondary refrigeration loop on the return lines to maintain tight fluid temperature tolerances before electrolyte re-enters the server racks.

    Peltier & Direct Metal Conduction: Leverages direct metal electrode conduction and optional Peltier-mode active cooling (depending on stack manufacturer specifications) for localized thermal tuning.

3. Gas Capture, Fluid Infrastructure & Safety

    Electrolysis Gas Capture: Captures hydrogen/oxygen gas off-gassing at both ends of the loop during charge and discharge cycles, recycling/reusing it to maximize overall system round-trip efficiency.

    Cabinet Flow Manifolds: Modular flow manifolds at each server cabinet feature precise fluidic control to dynamically tune both local power delivery and cooling capacity.

    Utilidor & Leak Protection: Employs protective utilidor conduit systems for fluid piping, offering robust secondary containment and leak prevention throughout the facility.
